01 / OVERVIEW

A map of AI for virtual power plants

Virtual power plants coordinate distributed energy resources such as solar generation, wind, batteries and electric vehicles. This review organizes AI approaches around the operational tasks they support, connecting methodological choices with system architecture and deployment challenges.

This companion guide summarizes the published review. Consult the paper for the detailed discussion, evidence and references. The guide is not an additional research publication.

Read the published abstract

The rapid proliferation of distributed energy resources (DERs), including photovoltaics, wind power, battery energy storage, and electric vehicles, has transformed traditional power systems into highly decentralized and data-rich environments. Virtual power plants (VPPs) have emerged as a key mechanism for aggregating these heterogeneous assets and enabling coordinated control, market participation, and grid-support functions. Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) have further elevated the scalability, autonomy, and responsiveness of VPP operations. This paper presents a comprehensive review of AI for VPPs, organized around a taxonomy of machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, and hybrid approaches, and examines how these methods map to core VPP functions such as forecasting, scheduling, market bidding, aggregation, and ancillary services. In parallel, we analyze enabling architectural frameworks—including centralized cloud, distributed edge, hybrid cloud–edge collaboration, and emerging 5G/LEO satellite communication infrastructures—that support real-time data exchange and scalable deployment of intelligent control. By integrating methodological, functional, and architectural perspectives, this review highlights the evolution of VPPs from rule-based coordination to intelligent, autonomous energy ecosystems. Key research challenges are identified in data quality, model interpretability, multi-agent scalability, cyber-physical resilience, and the integration of AI with digital twins and edge-native computation. These findings outline promising directions for next-generation intelligent VPPs capable of delivering secure, flexible, and self-optimizing DER aggregation at scale.

02 / READING GUIDE

Start with your research question

How is AI used in virtual power plants?

The review covers forecasting, scheduling, market bidding, aggregation and ancillary services. Use the methodological taxonomy in Section 3 to orient yourself, then read Section 4 for the discussion of VPP functions.

Method families discussed in Section 3
Method familyRole discussed in the review
Machine learningData-driven prediction from operational and market information.
Deep learningLearning complex temporal and spatial patterns.
Reinforcement learningSequential decisions for control, scheduling and market participation.
Hybrid approachesCombining learning, optimization and control methods.

These categories organize the literature; they do not establish a universal ranking of algorithms. Performance claims should be checked against the original studies and their assumptions.

Where should I start with reinforcement learning for VPP scheduling?

Begin with the reinforcement-learning discussion in Section 3 and the scheduling discussion in Section 4. When following the cited studies, compare their objectives, constraints, uncertainty assumptions and evaluation settings before choosing a method.

What makes deployment challenging?

The review discusses data quality, interpretability, coordination at scale and cyber-physical resilience, alongside cloud, edge and communication architectures. Section 6 sets out research challenges and future directions.

Source for this guide: Li, Wang & Liu (2026). Specific experimental findings remain attributable to the original studies cited in the review.
